# Oulton Park (International)

Oulton Park’s International loop threads through the former Oulton Estate, a swath of Cheshire parkland that club volunteers from the Mid-Cheshire Car Club converted into asphalt in 1953 so the North West could host its own major races.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oulton_Park">Oulton Park — Wikipedia</a></sup> The circuit still feels like it was draped over an English country estate: deer-fence-lined straights, blind crests beneath oak canopies, and folk tales like Knickerbrook—which allegedly earned its name when Blaster Bates’ stump-clearing dynamite startled a courting couple into abandoning their undergarments. Modern operators MotorSport Vision have kept the layout’s undulating character intact, so touring cars, superbikes, and the historic Gold Cup all return each year for the high-speed roller coaster.

What makes the International layout wicked is the compound gradient changes. From Old Hall you plunge through Cascades, skim Island Bend, then climb Clay Hill toward Druids without ever seeing the exit of the next corner until you’re committed.<sup><a href="https://www.iracing.com/tracks/oulton-park-circuit/">Oulton Park — iRacing.com</a></sup> The surface is smooth, but the camber constantly shifts from positive to off-camber, especially under the trees where light flickers across the windscreen. Every braking zone is either downhill or crested, so the car wants to go light just as you trail off the pedal. There are only two meaningful straights; momentum through medium-speed kinks is what sets up passes into Hislop’s and Lodge.

In the Ray FF1600, start conservative with around 53% brake bias to keep the rear planted when you crest Clay Hill. As grip builds, easing a half-click rearward lets the car rotate into Shell Oils Hairpin without binding up the front tires. Spend early sessions drilling the Cascades-to-Island sequence in third gear, aiming to release the brake in one smooth motion so the car floats to the inside curb. Once rhythm is there, run 15-lap stints at sunset to practice throttle modulation when long shadows mask apexes—exactly the kind of sight challenge that triggers lockups on race night. Treat every lap as one flowing ribbon and Oulton rewards you with a lap time that tumbles in five-tenths chunks; force it, and the track bites with understeer that feels like driving into molasses.
