# Motorsport Arena Oschersleben Grand Prix Course

When Oschersleben opened in 1997—Germany’s first purpose-built circuit since the post-war big three—it gave DTM, FIA GT and WTCC a modern, fan-friendly venue on the Magdeburg plain.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport_Arena_Oschersleben">Motorsport Arena Oschersleben — Wikipedia</a></sup> The arena’s flat, billiard-table asphalt hides surprise complexity: tight first-gear hairpins bookend long, flowing sequences, while the McDonald’s chicane provides the signature risk-reward moment that has launched more highlight reels than any other corner in German touring cars. Local folklore includes John Cleland’s “dark room” rant about the Turn 1 design, earned after yet another WTCC pileup.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport_Arena_Oschersleben#The_circuit">Motorsport Arena Oschersleben — The circuit</a></sup>

The Grand Prix lap snaps from discipline to discipline. You rip down the start straight, throw the car into the slow 90-degree Hotel corner, then sprint through a fast right-left that rewards patience on throttle. The back half is all about rhythm: Shell-Kurve’s double apex tempts early turn-in, while the McDonald’s chicane punishes greed with sausage curbs that happily snap a wishbone. Because elevation change is minimal, reference points are painted kerbs, marshal posts and braking boards—precision rather than bravery unlocks lap time.

In iRacing the Ray FF1600 thrives on that precision. The car hits fifth before the first braking zone, so managing the initial downshift cascade without locking the rears is key; bias around 53% keeps things stable over the slight crest. Roll second gear through Hotel and focus on unwinding the wheel early to maximise the short chute to Turn 3. The McDonald’s chicane is where races are won: you can straight-line the opening left by stealing a tyre-width of kerb, but you must square the car before the right-hand exit or the rear will skate onto the astroturf and cost half a second. Because the FF rewards momentum, treat the final triple left as one long arc rather than three distinct corners—float third gear, breathe the throttle once, and let the car track to the outer paint.

To prepare, build sessions that stress accuracy. Start with 15 minutes of “slow-in, fast-out” laps focusing solely on keeping two wheels within the painted bounds of the McDonald’s chicane; set a self-imposed penalty lap any time you monster a kerb. Next, run long stints in traffic (AI or ghosts) to rehearse defensive lines into Turn 1—setting up a late apex without parking the car mid-corner is a skill you’ll need in official races. Finish with qualifying simulations at late-afternoon lighting, when low sun hides the braking boards; mastering visual cues on the pit wall and grandstands ensures you’re not dependent on shadows. Consistency here keeps you out of the Turn 1 chaos and lets the Ray’s flow carry you onto the DTM scoreboard.
