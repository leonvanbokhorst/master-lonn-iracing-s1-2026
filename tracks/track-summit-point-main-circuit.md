# Summit Point Raceway Main Circuit

The Main Circuit is the one that started it all at Summit Point. Pat Goodman carved the original 2.0-mile layout out of farmland in 1969 as a home for SCCA club racing and federal driver training, long before the Jefferson and Shenandoah extensions existed.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_Point_Motorsports_Park">Summit Point Motorsports Park — Wikipedia</a></sup> The Carousel complex was grafted on in the early ’70s, transforming what was once a simple eight-turn loop into a flowing 10-corner lap that hosted IMSA GT, Can-Am, Trans-Am and today’s Jefferson 500 vintage festival.

Summit Point Main is deceptively fast. The lap begins with a downhill brake into Turn 1, a late-apex right that sets up the flat-out Turn 2 kink and the long climb to the Carousel. That double-apex left-hander rewards patience: trail the brake to tightline the first hook, then let the car breathe to the outer kerb before diving back across for Turn 7. From there the circuit plunges through the Esses—a sequence that can be taken nearly flat in a lightweight formula car—before spitting you into the heavy-braking Turn 10 and the short front straight. Elevation change and guardrails lined with trees give the place a vintage club-racing vibe; every apex is a commitment.

In iRacing the Ray FF1600 comes alive here. You can carry fourth gear through Turn 1 with just a brush of brake, provided you rotate the car early and let it track all the way to the exit curbing. The Carousel demands discipline: enter in third, bleed the brake until the nose finds the first apex, then roll maintenance throttle so the car stays balanced while you arc across to the second apex. Through the Esses, resist the temptation to saw at the wheel; a smooth steering trace keeps the Ray planted. For Turn 10, brake in a straight line at the 200 board, downshift to second, and be ready for the rear to lighten as the track drops away mid-corner—slightly higher brake bias (52–53%) keeps the car honest.

Dial in the lap with focused sessions. Start with ten-lap runs attacking only Turns 1–5, using telemetry to chase higher minimum speeds through Turn 3 without sacrificing traction. Next, run full-fuel stints to practice managing traffic in the Esses and defending inside into Turn 10—Summit’s best passing zone. Cap the week with qualifying sims at sunrise, when low light hides the Turn 1 brake markers; if you can hit your references then, race-day visibility will feel easy. Conquer the Carousel and the Ray will reward you with lap times that tumble in big chunks.
