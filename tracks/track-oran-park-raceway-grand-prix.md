# Oran Park Raceway Grand Prix Loop

Oran Park was Sydney’s suburban coliseum: carved into Dan Cleary’s farmland in 1962, expanded in the 1970s with a figure-eight bridge, and ultimately consumed by housing in 2010.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oran_Park_Raceway">Oran Park Raceway — Wikipedia</a></sup> In its heyday the Grand Prix layout hosted Tasman rounds, touring-car blockbusters, two runnings of the Australian Grand Prix and the final fireworks of Australian V8 Supercars. Fans packed the grassy mounds because you could see almost the entire lap from a single spot, watching cockatoos scatter as Formula 5000s thundered over the bridge.

The lap itself was a rollercoaster of camber changes despite minimal elevation. You attacked the downhill Pits Bend, plunged into the tight Coca-Cola corner, then climbed towards the bridge before flipping left-right through Revett. Crossing over your own braking line at full song felt surreal, and the final double-apex Suttons corner rewarded bravery: kiss the inside ripple strip and you’d rocket onto the short front straight; miss it and you’d bog down before the start/finish kink. The surface evolved over decades, so bumps in braking zones were folklore—every local knew which patch of tar to avoid.

In iRacing, Oran Park is a love letter to momentum cars like the Ray FF1600. With no long straights, every mistake compounds: over-slow Coca-Cola and you crawl all the way to the bridge. Focus on rolling second gear with minimal steering angle, then upshift early as you climb; keeping the engine in the meat of the torque curve matters more than redlining. The bridge approach is blind, so use the catch fencing on drivers’ left as your braking trigger, and line the car up centre-track to soften the landing. For the final corner, trail the brake to the first apex, breathe the throttle mid-turn, then release the steering to capture the second apex and carry third gear to the line.

Practice should feel like time travel. Start with rhythm runs: string together 20 laps, concentrating on linking Castrol, the bridge and Revett without lifting off throttle more than once. Next, simulate traffic—Oran Park’s narrow ribbon demands decisive moves, so rehearse passes into Suttons that rely on exit drive rather than divebombs. Finally, honour the circuit’s sunset farewell by running sessions in late-afternoon sim lighting; as the sun drops behind the bridge, reference points disappear, forcing you to rely on muscle memory just like the heroes who closed the park in 2010.
