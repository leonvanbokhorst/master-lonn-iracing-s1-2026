# Okayama International Circuit (Full Course)

Okayama’s story starts in Japan’s bubble economy, when entrepreneur Hajime Tanaka carved a motorsport playground into rolling tea-country hills in 1990 and invited friends to thrash Ferraris between rounds of golf.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okayama_International_Circuit">Okayama International Circuit — Wikipedia</a></sup> The “TI Circuit Aida” country-club vibe never stopped big-time events from visiting; Michael Schumacher swept the Pacific Grands Prix here in ’94 and ’95, cementing the venue’s place in F1 trivia. Today the rebranded Okayama International Circuit pulls Super GT, Super Formula Lights, and GT World Challenge Asia—proof that the 13-turn ribbon still punches above its rural weight.

On paper the lap looks simple—two medium-length straights bracketing linked complexes—but there’s a rhythm to mastering its crests and fall-away exits.<sup><a href="https://www.iracing.com/tracks/okayama-international-circuit/">Okayama International Circuit — iRacing.com</a></sup> Turns like Atwood curve ask you to brake downhill, roll mid-corner speed forever, then breathe the throttle so you don’t light up rears over the hill onto the back straight. The final triple-right—Redman, Hobbs, Revolver—demands inch-perfect trail braking to keep the car balanced as elevation see-saws under the tires. Misjudge one release and you lose momentum all the way to the finish line, which is why Japanese juniors spend whole winters grinding sessions here.

In the Ray FF1600 the key is discipline. Start with 53% brake bias to keep the rear planted into the downhill Turn 1 braking zone; as confidence builds, back it off half a click so the car rotates into Atwood without washing wide. Build drills around two anchors: first, string together pipeline laps focusing solely on the hairpins, chasing identical minimum speeds lap after lap; second, run 12-lap stints in rubbered-in afternoon conditions so you learn how the FF1600 responds as the track grips up and the tail starts to chatter through Revolver. Close each session with two cool-down laps focusing on throttle smoothing—mastery here is less about heroics and more about painting the same arc every time. Nail that, and Okayama stops feeling like a polite country club and becomes a relentless momentum test that rewards your smoothest driving.
