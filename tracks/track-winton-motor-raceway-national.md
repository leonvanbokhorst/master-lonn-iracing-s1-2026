# Winton Motor Raceway (National)

Winton was born from Benalla Auto Club’s desire for a permanent home: by late 1960 club volunteers had carved a ribbon of bitumen out of dairy country, and on 26 November 1961 Victoria’s newest circuit opened to a 10,000-strong crowd.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winton_Motor_Raceway">Winton Motor Raceway — Wikipedia</a></sup> The layout grew in 1997, stretching past the old esses to form today’s 3.0 km National Circuit while the original “club” loop survived inside. Touring cars, Formula Ford, Australian Drivers’ Championship rounds, even Supercars’ beloved Winton SuperSprint—everything visits the self-styled “Australia’s Action Track,” famed for tight sightlines and bumper-to-bumper racing.

Despite its short lap, Winton never stops asking questions. The surface stitches together short straights with off-camber switchbacks, which is why Dick Johnson famously likened it to “running a marathon around your clothes-line.” Twelve corners keep the car oscillating between second and third gear, and the bumps under braking into Turn 3 and Turn 10 punish anyone who doesn’t straighten the wheel before asking for pedal pressure. The infield is flat farmland, yet wind whipping across the hay bales changes braking references by metres, so local heroes learn to trust the paint on pit buildings more than trackside boards.

The Ray FF1600 suits Winton’s stop-start nature in iRacing, but you have to guard momentum jealously. The key is finessing rotation into the Turn 2 hairpin and the double right at Turn 7 so you can launch onto the kinked back straight; over-slow either and you’ll spend 10 seconds watching the delta fade. Because the car is light, it rides the National Circuit’s curbing well—modulating the throttle to keep weight settled lets you mount the inside curb at Turn 6 without unsettling the rear. Bias around 52% keeps the nose planted over the Turn 3 bumps, although edging rearward half a click late in a run helps the car change direction through the fast Turn 11 sweeper once the Cooper tires warm.

To prepare, break the lap into two drills. First, run five-lap stints focusing solely on turns 1–6 and use telemetry to chase minimum-speed parity between the two hairpins; aim for less than 2 km/h variance. Second, practice racecraft by setting up AI traffic or ghost laps that force you to pass into Turn 10—the only “big brake” zone where lining up a cutback actually works. Finish the week with full-fuel stints in sunset conditions so you can rehearse dealing with glare off the windscreen, mirroring the real-world challenge of late-afternoon Australian rounds. Keep the rhythm smooth and Winton transforms from a claustrophobic clothes-line into a flowing ribbon where every apex sets up the next attack.
