# VIR North Course

VIR’s rebirth in 2000 restored more than just the storied Full Course—it gave the facility modular loops like the 2.25-mile North Course, which threads together the track’s greatest hits: the Horseshoe, the Snake, the Climbing Esses, Oak Tree and Hog Pen.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_International_Raceway">Virginia International Raceway — Wikipedia</a></sup> North uses the same front straight and iconic back section as the full layout, trimmed with a short connector after Oak Tree that loops you back toward pit lane. It’s the configuration most often used for MotoAmerica and testing because it packs VIR’s rhythm into a tighter lap.

North starts with the Horseshoe’s double-apex right-left, dives through the Snake and into the ferocious Climbing Esses: a left-right-left-right sequence taken flat in momentum cars as the track crests and falls away. Oak Tree (now missing its signature oak) is still the slowest point on the course—get it right and you rocket onto the long back straight. The Roller Coaster and Hog Pen finish the lap with a downhill plunge and off-camber braking that tests patience as much as bravery. Elevation swings of 130 feet plus blind apexes mean every lap feels like a fight between commitment and restraint.

In iRacing the Ray FF1600 rewards quiet hands here. Trail brake gently into the Horseshoe, double-apexing the right so you can straighten the wheel early and carry speed across the short chute. Through the Climbing Esses, pick a high gear (fourth) and keep the steering trace smooth; a mid-corner lift unsettles the car more than a small slide. Oak Tree is second gear—modulate the brake on entry, late apex and feed throttle the moment you see sky under the bridge. Hog Pen is all about balance: downshift to third, maintain a whisper of throttle to keep the rear planted, then unwind the wheel early so you can use every inch of exit curb without dropping wheels.

To master VIR North, structure practice around rhythm. Start with sector drills from the Snake through Oak Tree, aiming to keep throttle application above 60% across the Esses without triggering a 1x in iRacing. Next, run long stints to learn how the Ray’s tires respond on the back straight—late-session slipstream battles are common here. Finish with qualifying sims at sunset; as the light falls, the Esses become silhouettes, forcing you to rely on muscle memory and peripheral cues. Nail those, and VIR North transforms from intimidating rollercoaster to a lap you can dance with.
