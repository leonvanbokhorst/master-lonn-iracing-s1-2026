# Circuito de Navarra (Speed Circuit – Medium)

Circuito de Navarra rose out of the Rioja vineyards in 2010 as Spain’s newest FIA Grade 2 playground, and its medium “Speed Circuit” configuration is the current go-to for tighter sprint racing.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuito_de_Navarra">Circuito de Navarra — Wikipedia</a></sup> When MotorSport Vision bought the venue in 2022 they resurfaced the place, realigned Turn 6, and in 2024 finished the extension that created today’s 2.693 km medium loop—a compact ribbon where local GT-CER racers, Ferrari Challenge UK visitors, and Spain’s ESBK superbikes dice for trophies within sight of the Sierra de Codés ridgeline.

The medium layout keeps Navarra’s trademark mix of elevation and long-radius bends but removes the longer back stretch, forcing you to link four distinct rhythm zones: the fast Turn 1–2 sweep, the uphill brake into the corkscrew-style Turn 5, the technical stadium of Turns 6–8, and the traction-limited double right that fires you back onto the pit straight.<sup><a href="https://www.iracing.com/tracks/circuito-de-navarra/">Circuito de Navarra — iRacing.com</a></sup> The asphalt is billiard-smooth after the MSV resurfacing, which means you can be greedier with minimum speed, but crosswinds funneling through the plains will still nudge the car toward the gravel if you exit wide.

In the Ray FF1600 treat Navarra like a momentum medley. Begin with a neutral 52.5% brake bias so the car stays planted through the downhill brake-in to Turn 5; as tires come up to temp shift half a percent rearward to help rotation in the slower stadium section. Focus practice on carrying third-gear roll speed through Turns 6 and 7—if you can keep steering inputs minimal and maintain 105 km/h, the short chute to Turn 8 rewards you with a clean defensive line. Finish sessions with standing-start drills: the grid sits on a slight camber change, and launching without wheelspin saves the clutch for the inevitable lap-one swarm. Do this and Navarra’s medium loop transforms from a stop-start chore into a flowing Iberian kart track scaled for single-seaters.
